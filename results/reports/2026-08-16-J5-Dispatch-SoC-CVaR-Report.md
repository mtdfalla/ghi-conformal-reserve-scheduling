## Model — load-independent cost of uncertainty with a finite battery

The operator counts on a PV quantity derived from a forecast quantile — that is the
reserve decision — and pre-commits backup accordingly. Realized PV then differs from the
counted PV, and **only that imbalance is priced**: no site load is modelled, assumed or
required anywhere in the cost. A finite battery (SoC dynamics, one-way efficiency,
power and energy limits) stores surplus to cover later deficits; residual deficit is
charged at the fast-diesel/VOLL premium `c_u` and surplus beyond battery room at `c_o`.
The oracle counts on the realized value and therefore costs exactly zero, which is what
makes "value captured" well posed. SoC resets each morning, so the **daily operating
cost is the CVaR unit**.

- Parameters: `c_o` = 0.30 $/kWh, `c_u`/`c_o` = 10; battery default **E_max = 600 kWh,
  P_max = 400 kW**, round-trip η ≈ 0.90, SoC ∈ [0.10, 1.0]; sensitivity over
  {small 250 kWh / 250 kW, large 1500 kWh / 600 kW}.
- Policies: deterministic (point), ICP, Mondrian, Mondrian-CQR, oracle. Each CP policy
  sweeps a reserve level ρ, tracing the (expected cost, CVaR₀.₉₅) frontier.
- Code: `03_code/dispatch/dispatch_soc.py`, `03_code/_j5_aggregate.py`.

**Key findings**

1. **Regime-aware uncertainty captures most of the achievable value.** Mondrian-CQR
   closes 94.1 % of the deterministic→oracle expected-cost gap at 5 min and 86.5 % at 30 min,
   against marginal ICP's 90.8 % and 80.5 %.
2. **The advantage is largest on tail risk.** On CVaR₀.₉₅ — the worst 5 % of days —
   Mondrian-CQR captures 86.5 % and 83.8 % against ICP's 74.6 % and 68.8 %. Regime-conditional plus
   quantile calibration disproportionately cuts the expensive bad-day events. This is the
   robustness story: calibrated uncertainty buys stability, not only average savings.
3. **The cost-minimising reserve level is method-specific.** It is ρ = 0.80 for the
   regime-aware methods against ρ = 0.90 for marginal ICP, which must over-reserve to
   compensate for its transitional miscalibration.

A bigger battery hedges forecast error, so absolute costs fall — but **calibrated
regime-aware reserve stays decisive at every size**, and matters most when storage is
scarce: with the small battery at 5 min it cuts cost by a factor of 5.1, against 15.0 with
the large one. Deterministic cost is nearly battery-insensitive because its errors are
frequent and two-sided.

## Notes / limitations

- Load-independent by construction. A full unit commitment would need a real
  site-load series, which this project does not have; assuming one would make the
  reported cost a property of the assumption rather than of the forecast.
- Real PV scatter beyond f(GHI) is treated as non-forecastable noise, which biases all
  policies identically. CVaR level 0.95.
- The θ-selection caveat at the top of this report is the reason these numbers are not
  the article's.
